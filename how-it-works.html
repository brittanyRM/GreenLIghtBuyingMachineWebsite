The program

How it works

Ten stages from qualification to buyer placement. You run the renovation. We handle the two hardest parts — finding the deal and finding the buyer.

It's a flip. The exit is what changes.

You buy the distressed house and finance it the way you always do — first, second, hard money, your usual stack. You run your crew, you hold the budget, you own the risk. Nothing about the acquisition or the buildout is unfamiliar.

What changes is who you sell to. A retail buyer prices your house against comps down the street. A co-living investor prices it against what the finished house produces by the room, and that's a different number. You're buying for the potential the property will have, and selling to someone who's paying for exactly that.

The other change is that we're identifying your buyer while you're still working. You're not listing and hoping.

The Green Light Way

The floor is eight bedrooms and two bathrooms. We don't build below it, because room count is where the economics come from. Everything above that floor is a decision the numbers make for you.

Bedrooms don't tell you what a co-living house is worth. Rentable rooms do — and not every room is worth the same. PadSplit reports what rooms actually rent for by submarket, and a room with its own ensuite bathroom prices differently than one sharing down the hall.

That turns bathroom count into an underwriting decision rather than a finish preference. We run that math on distressed inventory before anyone walks a property, so students see houses already screened for room count, ensuite potential, and margin instead of guessing at a listing.

It's also why we start from scratch rather than converting an existing rental. Adding bathrooms is the kind of thing you can only plan from the first wall — retrofit it later and you're working around plumbing that was never meant to be there.

That's the Green Light Way: buy on what the house can become, build it to the standard the buyer expects, and know who that buyer is before you finish. The margin lives in the specific deal, not in an average we could print here.
