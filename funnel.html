The retail exit keeps getting harder

You're bidding against every other flipper in the Valley for the same inventory, then selling into the same retail buyer pool. Rates moved, margins thinned, days on market stretched. None of that is news to you.

The house itself isn't the problem. The buyer is. A family with a mortgage will pay what the comps say, and the comps are set by houses that produce nothing.

An investor buying a co-living property is doing different math. They're pricing what the house earns by the room, and a well-built eight- or ten-bedroom conversion earns considerably more than a three-bedroom rental on the same footprint.

That's the whole idea. Build for a buyer who values the property on income instead of comps.

We’ll teach you the way we build

An investor pricing a house on room income is a different buyer than a family pricing it on comps. They know what they’re looking at, which means the build has to hold up to someone who does this for a living. Almost nothing about your process changes. What you produce at the end does.
