Before you apply

Is it for you?

This isn't a starter program. Being honest about that up front saves everyone a hard conversation three weeks in.

A fit if you

  • Have finished renovations you can point to
  • Run your own crew or have trades you trust
  • Can read a scope and hold a budget
  • Have capital or reliable access to it
  • Want a different exit, not a different hobby
  • Can work an Arizona property

Not a fit if you

  • Haven't completed a renovation yet
  • Are looking for passive income
  • Want to assign the contract instead of building
  • Need someone else to manage the build
  • Are shopping for a guarantee

What we expect from you

  • You build to the standard, not to your usual retail spec
  • You keep the portal current so we can see where the deal stands
  • You raise problems early, while they're still cheap
  • You show up to cohort calls
  • You make decisions on your own project — we advise, you own it

Straight answers

Do I have to use your deal?

No. Bring your own if you have one — we'll underwrite it with you. Sourcing is there because finding the right house is the part most people get wrong, not because it's mandatory. Confirm this is accurate.

What if the renovation goes sideways?

It's your project and your capital, so the risk is real. What the program changes is that you have people who have done this before looking at the same problem, and checkpoints designed to catch trouble before it compounds.

Is the buyer guaranteed?

Answer this precisely with Brian and Gina, and have counsel review the wording. Buyer placement is a core part of the model, and how it's described here needs to match exactly what's contractually promised.

Do I have to be in Arizona?

You don't have to live here, but the property does. Cohorts are Arizona-based and the deals we source are in this market.

How long does one project take?

Add a realistic range from completed student projects.

Send us a property. We'll tell you if it converts.

No charge, no pitch. Send an address and we'll come back with whether the house works for co-living, roughly what room count it supports, and what the conversion would involve. If it's a no, we'll tell you why.
